How Long Drying Takes in a Columbia Home

Touch is a poor test for whether a wall or floor has dried. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. In Columbia we use pin meters that measure moisture inside wood and drywall, non-invasive meters that scan behind surfaces, and thermal cameras that show cool, damp patches.

Different materials let go of water at different speeds. Carpet and drywall respond quickly, while framing lumber, subfloor, hardwood and concrete hold on much longer. We place air movers and dehumidifiers according to those differences and adjust them as the daily readings change. Most jobs finish in 3 to 5 days, and if yours will take longer you will hear it from us at the start.

The expensive error is pulling equipment too soon. Materials closed up while still damp can grow mold, swell, or come loose months later, long after the job seemed finished. Every Columbia job is therefore dried to a recorded target, and the log stays on file.

Water Damage We See Most in Columbia

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Here is what homeowners around Columbia phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: Small drips from a worn fitting or a tiny hole in a pipe may go unnoticed behind drywall for a long time. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Porous items it touches, such as carpet and pad, generally have to be removed.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and Columbia properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
