Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in O'Fallon

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Water that came up through the bowl is not, and it has to be handled as contaminated.

Our O'Fallon crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Insurance

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
  • Bowl water pushed over the rim by a clog in the drain line or the trap.
  • Tank parts, the fill valve or the flapper, that jam and let water run nonstop.
  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Soaked Subfloor

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. Once wet, the plywood subfloor swells, its layers separate, and the floor covering bonded on top can come loose.

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

Drywall edges and the vanity base stand in the water and draw it up like a wick. Paint may look normal even when the wall is damp several inches up from the floor.

Odor That Returns

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

The bottom edge of a baseboard or door casing draws in water as soon as the floor floods. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Toilet Overflow Cleanup

1

Identify the Category

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extract and Remove

Our crew removes the standing water, takes out any baseboard, drywall or carpet pad that is contaminated, and pulls the toilet if water got below its base and the flange.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

We clean the subfloor, fixtures, wall bases and floors, then treat them with antimicrobial products. For Category 3 jobs, disinfection comes first and drying equipment starts only afterward.

4

Drying and Final Readings

The bathroom, plus any room below that got wet, is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers. Our crew keeps metering the subfloor and the ceiling until both read normal again.
