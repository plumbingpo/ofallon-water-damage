Bathroom Water Damage in O'Fallon

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. We help O'Fallon homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Signs to Look For

  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little

How We Fix It

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Slow Vanity Drips

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.
